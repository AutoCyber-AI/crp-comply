## 1. THE UNIFYING INSIGHT (why one variable spans both products)

Every major AI assurance framework — **EU AI Act, AIUC-1, ISO/IEC 42001, NIST AI RMF,
SOC 2-for-AI** — underneath its own vocabulary demands the same three things:

1. **Controls EXIST** — you have safety, security, and governance mechanisms.
2. **Controls OPERATE** — they run on every AI call, continuously, not on paper.
3. **You can PROVE it** — verifiable evidence the controls ran, not assertions.

Most organisations manage #1 and sometimes #2. **The universal failure is #3** — proving,
continuously and verifiably, that the controls actually operated. That is precisely and
uniquely what CRP produces: **every governed AI call emits HMAC-signed, tamper-evident
evidence that the control ran, who authorised it, and what it decided.**

> CRP is the evidence layer for AI security and safety. Whatever standard holds you —
> EU AI Act, AIUC-1, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF — CRP proves your controls are in place and
> operating, from live runtime data, signed and verifiable.

This is the shared variable. Both hero sections express THIS, then name the flagship
standards (EU AI Act + AIUC-1) as proof points.

## 2. WHAT THE MAJOR STANDARDS REQUIRE — AND THE COMMON DEMAND

| Framework | What it is | What it demands (the common thread) |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------------------------|
| **EU AI Act** | Law (Reg. 2024/1689) | Risk mgmt, logging (Art.12), human oversight (Art.14), QMS (Art.17), technical documentation (Annex IV) — i.e. **evidence the controls operate** |
| **AIUC-1** | The first AI-agent security/safety/reliability standard (AIUC; audited by Schellman; quarterly updates). 6 domains: Data&Privacy, Security, Safety, Reliability, Accountability, Society | Adversarial testing, real-time input filtering, access control, **audit trails**, continuous monitoring — i.e. **evidence the agent behaves under pressure** |
| **ISO/IEC 42001** | AI management system standard | Documented controls + operational evidence + continual improvement |
| **NIST AI RMF** | US risk framework | Govern/Map/Measure/Manage — measurement & documentation of AI risk controls |
| **SOC 2 (AI)** | Trust services | Evidence controls operate over a period, not a point in time |

**The common requirement across all of them: provable, operating controls — continuous,
verifiable evidence.** CRP is built to emit exactly that. Different standards, one evidence
engine.

## 7. HONEST BOUNDARIES (keep, for credibility)

State these plainly — they protect the whole positioning from a skeptical auditor:
- CRP produces the **technical control evidence**; it is **not itself an accredited
  certifier**. AIUC-1 certificates are issued by AIUC via accredited auditors (e.g.
  Schellman); EU AI Act conformity is assessed by notified bodies / self-assessment per risk
  class. CRP makes you **audit-ready and certification-ready**; it does not issue the
  certificate. Say "AIUC-1-ready / evidence-ready," never "AIUC-1 certified" (unless you
  pursue and obtain it for your own products).
- AIUC-1 **updates quarterly** (the Q2 2026 refresh focused on MCP security, third-party
  risk, agent identity/permissions). Position CRP's **continuous** evidence as the fit for a
  fast-moving standard — but commit to tracking the requirement changes; a static mapping
  goes stale. **[ACTION: keep the control-evidence mapping versioned against AIUC-1 releases.]**
- Some requirements (e.g. third-party attestation, catastrophic-misuse modules) are NOT
  things a control-evidence layer provides alone. Show them in the mapping as "via accredited
  partner" rather than claiming coverage. Honesty here is what an auditor respects.
- The numbers (13-stage, <50ms, control counts) must be reproducible and current before
  amplifying — auditors and CISOs WILL probe them.
